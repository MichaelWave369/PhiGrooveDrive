Finite rectangular electrodes. Fixed voltages. A grounded external enclosure. We calculate a two-dimensional potential, then independently integrate Maxwell stress around each conductor, the pair, and the enclosure. The result is an accounting experiment, not a propulsion demonstration.

SIMULATION ONLY. This is a planar uniform-vacuum model, not a physical T-Blade replication, validation of the Exodus Effect, or a 3D thruster. Forces are in µN per meter of assumed out-of-plane depth, not total 3D force. A nonzero force on the electrode pair can be balanced by its grounded enclosure.

METHOD & CLAIM BOUNDARY

What this actually establishes

The planar potential is found using linear triangular Galerkin elements, then closed-contour forces are estimated using the electrostatic Maxwell stress tensor. The force on the electrode pair is compared against a separately integrated contour near the grounded box.

A potential difference supplies energy, not an exemption from momentum conservation. A grounded enclosure is an external body that can exchange force with the electrodes.

Known limitations: fixed potentials, perfect conductors, vacuum dielectric, no space charge, no gas, no cables, no heating, no time-varying fields, no 3D edge effects. Coarse grids can produce path-dependent contour errors.
